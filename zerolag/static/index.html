Decision rules

How a risk score becomes a decision

    Privacy and security controls

    • Tokenised identities. UPI IDs and device IDs are replaced by keyed HMAC-SHA256 tokens on arrival. The model and logs never see them.
    • Masked display. Analysts see masked IDs such as us***48@okaxis only.
    • No protected attributes. Age and other demographics are not used to make decisions.
    • Explainable by default. Every warning and hold lists the signals that caused it.
    • Human oversight. A hold pauses a payment; it never cancels it.

    Platform

    Where this console’s scoring engine is running

    How it works

    What happens in the few milliseconds between a customer pressing “Pay” and the money moving.

    The decision path

    1. Protect identities

      The payment arrives from the bank’s UPI system. Account and device IDs are tokenised immediately.

    2. Compare with normal behaviour

      14 signals are computed from the customer’s and the receiver’s live history: usual amounts, trusted receivers and devices, bursts of payments, how many people paid this receiver today, and whether the customer is on a call.

    3. Score the risk

      A gradient-boosted model trained on known scam patterns gives a risk score. A second model flags behaviour never seen in genuine traffic.

    4. Explain

      Each signal is reset to a normal value to measure how much it raised the risk. The strongest signals become the reasons shown here.

    5. Decide and inform

      The payment is allowed, warned or held. Warned and held customers get a message specific to the scam, in English and Hindi.

    Scams ZeroLag looks for

    • Digital arrest. A fake police or CBI officer keeps the victim on a call and directs transfers.
    • Collect-request scams. “Approve to receive a prize”. Approving actually sends money.
    • Account takeover. After a SIM swap, a new phone drains the account in minutes.
    • Money mules. Accounts that collect from many victims and pass the money on.

    Glossary

    Risk score
    The model’s estimate, from 0 to 100%, that the payment is a scam.
    Warned (step-up)
    The customer must read a warning and confirm before the payment proceeds.
    Held
    The payment is paused until the customer or bank verifies it.
    New receiver
    A receiver becomes trusted only 24 hours after the first payment to them.
    Novelty detector
    Flags combinations of behaviour that never occur in genuine traffic.
    Decision time
    Time to tokenise, compute signals, score, explain and decide one payment.
